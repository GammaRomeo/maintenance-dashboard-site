Why bother

Good photos become a tappable map of your vehicle — numbered pins on real photos of your engine bay, suspension corners, exhaust, or interior, each linked to a torque or maintenance spec. No purchased 3D model required: your own photos are the diagram.

Before you start

  • Vehicle off, cool, and stable — on level ground with the parking brake set, or on properly rated jack stands (never a jack alone) for anything underneath.
  • Let hot components (exhaust, turbo, engine) cool before getting close with a camera or your hands.
  • Take off loose clothing, jewelry, and lanyards before leaning into an engine bay.

How to shoot

  • Even light. Overcast daylight or shop lighting from two angles works best. A single harsh light throws hard shadows and blows out reflections on bare metal.
  • Wide, then close. One wide shot showing how the section fits into the whole vehicle, then close-ups of each fastener or component cluster.
  • Square-on, not angled. Keep the camera close to perpendicular to the surface — an angled or wide-angle shot distorts spacing.
  • Something for scale. A tape measure, a coin, or a known bolt head in frame helps confirm sizes later.
  • Clean first. Wipe off heavy grease or grime before shooting — it's usually hiding a stamped size or part number.
  • Zoom in on markings. Casting numbers, stamped sizes, color-coded heads, and OEM part numbers are worth their own close-up.
  • Multiple angles on anything 3D. A suspension corner or engine bracket usually needs a front, side, and top-down shot to be unambiguous later.

Zone by zone

Engine bay — one full-bay wide shot first, then section by section (intake side, exhaust side, front accessory drive, firewall side). Prop the hood securely before shooting.
Suspension / wheel corners — shoot with the wheel off if you're already doing other work; otherwise get as much of the strut/control-arm assembly as you can through the wheel well opening.
Exhaust — shoot in sections (manifold/downpipe, mid-pipe, muffler/tips); note any aftermarket sections since they may not match factory spacing.
Interior / body — seat mounting points, seatbelt anchors, and trim fasteners are usually hidden under covers or carpet edges. Photograph both the visible fastener and the cavity underneath if you pull a cover.

What happens to these photos

For now: nothing automatic. Keep them yourself. Once photo-pin navigation ships, they'll become the base layer for that vehicle's photo view — pins placed on them and linked to the spec data already researched for that platform.
